# Chapter 4: Advanced Antenna Systems

Let's say your transmitter generates 100 watts of RF power. How much of that power does the antenna radiate? In an ideal lossless system, the antenna radiates all 100 watts. In a poor installation, losses in the feed line and connections, compounded by a mismatch, might leave only 25 watts to radiate — with the other 75 watts being lost, mostly as heat.

The antenna gets the glory, but the connectors, feed line, and matching network can all have a major impact on your signal. Understanding this system helps you get your signal out efficiently, whether you're chasing DX—shorthand for distant or international stations—chatting on a local net, or experimenting with digital modes.

We'll start with how characteristic impedance affects your choice of feed line and what an SWR reading can tell you about the match. From there, we'll work through the practical choices for dipoles and verticals, then examine how directional antennas concentrate their signals. We'll also see how mobile and portable operators fit antennas into the space they have.

The goal isn't building the perfect antenna system—it's understanding how your system works so you can make informed choices, diagnose problems, and optimize for your situation. Great antennas don't require huge towers or massive budgets. They require understanding the physics and applying it thoughtfully.
