## Chapter 4: Signals and Modulation

A steady carrier can tell you that a transmitter is on, but it cannot carry a conversation until something changes. Modulation makes those changes follow a voice, a string of bits, or a picture. Each choice leaves a recognizable shape in the signal's spectrum.

The same few ideas connect these signals: mixing moves information to a new frequency, smooth transitions limit bandwidth, and codes tell the receiver what the changes mean. You can use those connections to make sense of an unfamiliar mode—and to find out why a transmitter sounds fine in its own monitor while taking up too much room on the band.
