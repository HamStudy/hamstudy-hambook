## Chapter 7: Radio Wave Propagation

A station beyond the horizon needs a route for its signal. That route might follow the ground, bend through the lower atmosphere, return from the ionosphere, or scatter from a meteor trail or the Moon. Each route has its own limits and favorable conditions.

Frequency and antenna direction help select a route; time and changing conditions decide whether it works. Propagation reports and forecasts give evidence about those conditions. When a band goes quiet, you have several things to investigate: the frequency, the route, the time, and whether anyone is transmitting. A propagation forecast gives you a place to start listening.
