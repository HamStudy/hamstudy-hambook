## Chapter 5: Digital Radio and Receivers

A receiver has to preserve a small wanted signal through every step between the antenna and the speaker. In a digital radio, some of those steps act on voltages and others act on numbers. The boundary between them matters: software cannot recover detail that the converter never captured.

Put a weak DX station beside a loud local signal and you ask two different things of the hardware. It must add little noise to weak signals, and it must remain well behaved when strong signals arrive nearby. Sampling, filtering, noise figure, and dynamic range describe how the receiver meets those demands.
