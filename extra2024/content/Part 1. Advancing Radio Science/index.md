# Part 1: Advancing Radio Science

A good SWR reading can hide an inefficient antenna. More receiver gain can make a weak station harder to hear when a strong neighbor causes overload. Familiar measurements and controls tell us more when we understand the behavior behind them.

This part takes that closer look. We begin with the properties that make one component suitable for a job and another a poor choice. AC calculations then help predict circuit behavior, from a filter's bandwidth to an amplifier's load. We use those ideas to follow signals through a radio, out through an antenna, and along a changing propagation path.

The math gives us ways to compare designs and predict results before trying them. We'll work through it as it becomes useful, keeping the circuit or signal in view. A calculated value should tell you something about the radio, not just fill a space on the page.
