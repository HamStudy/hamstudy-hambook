# Part 1: Advancing Radio Science

A receiver can pull one conversation out of a crowded band. An antenna can favor one direction while rejecting another. Both become easier to understand when you can follow what happens to a signal along the way.

We'll begin with components and logic, then explore AC circuits, where timing matters as much as voltage and current. From there, components become working circuits, circuits create and process signals, and antenna systems send those signals into the world. Propagation completes the path to another station.

Some of this will feel familiar; some will be new. When we put numbers to an idea, keep an eye on what the answer says about the radio. Does more capacitance move the resonance up or down? Where does the unwanted mixing product land? The calculation earns its place when it helps you predict what happens.
