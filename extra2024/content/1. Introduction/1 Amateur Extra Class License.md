# Amateur Extra Class License

Amateur Extra is the highest of the three US amateur license classes. It adds access to portions of several HF bands that General operators cannot use. Those extra frequencies can be welcome space during a contest or a chance to reach a station operating in an Extra-only segment.

Your radio won't gain any watts when the license arrives: the usual maximum power limit stays the same as for General. What you gain is more frequency choices, along with knowledge that can help you make better use of the station you have.

## What the Upgrade Opens

Amateur Extra licensees have all the frequency privileges available to US amateurs, subject to the rules for each band and location. They also qualify for additional call sign formats. A shorter call sign can be convenient, but keeping your current one is fine, too.

Helping others get licensed or upgrade is another reason to earn Extra. General-class Volunteer Examiners (VEs) can give Technician exams; only Extra-class VEs can give exams for all three license classes. *You must meet the examiner requirements and receive accreditation from a Volunteer Examiner Coordinator; the license alone doesn't make you an examiner.*

Extra also carries a bit of prestige in the ham radio world. Some people want the satisfaction of holding the highest license class or enjoy the challenge of earning it. Those are good reasons to upgrade, too.

## Knowledge You Can Put to Work

Some Extra topics explain a choice you'll face at the operating desk: which receiver filter to use, how to recognize overload, or why a digital mode gets through when voice does not. Others help you interpret an antenna analyzer or understand a circuit you want to build.

You don't have to build a radio to benefit from knowing how one works. A control labeled "RF gain" or a specification called "noise figure" means more when you know what happens to the signal. That knowledge also helps you describe a problem when you need advice.
