### Section 11.6: Operating Across Borders

Calling an amateur abroad raises questions about what you may say. Operating while you are abroad raises more: which country permits your station to operate, and what does it allow?

#### Contacting a Foreign Station

*An international contact can include radio experiments and personal conversation.* It does not have to be in English, nor is it reserved for emergency organizations.

> **Key Information:** Communications to amateur stations in foreign countries must be limited to those incidental to the purpose of the amateur service and remarks of a personal nature. {{< link id="E1C02" >}}

For third-party traffic, apply the separate arrangements discussed in the previous section. Permission to talk to a foreign amateur does not by itself settle whether you may carry a message for someone else.

#### Operating While Traveling

Before packing a transmitter, check whether the country you will visit recognizes your license. Find out which bands and modes you may use, which documents to bring, and how to identify. Check these details before you arrive with a charged battery and an antenna ready to go. Your US band privileges do not travel unchanged.

> **Key Information:** CEPT allows eligible FCC-licensed US citizens to operate in many European countries and eligible amateurs from many European countries to operate in the United States. {{< link id="E1C11" >}}

CEPT is the European Conference of Postal and Telecommunications Administrations. *Its agreements let eligible visitors use their home licenses in countries that take part.* Both the country list and local rules can change. Check [CEPT's current T/R 61-01 documents](https://docdb.cept.org/document/related/926) and the host country's regulator before your visit.

*Keep the [FCC's CEPT public notice](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-16-1048A1_Rcd.pdf) with the documents you plan to carry.* It identifies the US license classes covered and the evidence you must have with you.

> **Key Information:** Operation under CEPT rules abroad, where permitted, requires a copy of FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048. {{< link id="E1C06" >}}

Also carry proof of US citizenship and of your FCC license grant. Follow the host country's call-sign format; “/CEPT” is not a universal suffix. An Amateur Extra license qualifies for the full CEPT radio-amateur license category. The host's rules still apply.

The Americas have a separate permit arrangement.

> **Key Information:** An International Amateur Radio Permit, or IARP, allows US amateurs to operate in certain countries of the Americas. {{< link id="E1C04" >}}

Check that your destination takes part, and get the required permit before using this route. IARP is not a worldwide license or another name for CEPT.

#### A Canadian License in the United States

An eligible visitor may operate under the US–Canadian reciprocal arrangement. *Start with the Canadian license's terms, then apply the US rules.* Both sets of limits matter. Use the privileges they have in common rather than choosing whichever rule is more generous.

> **Key Information:** A person operating in the United States under a Canadian amateur license has that license's operating terms and conditions, not exceeding US Amateur Extra privileges. {{< link id="E1F02" >}}

For example, permission to use a mode at home does not let a visitor use it where US band rules prohibit it. The agreement permits operation during the visit; local limits still govern the station.

#### Aboard a Vessel or Aircraft

*Aboard a US-registered vessel in international waters, your US amateur license supplies the amateur operating privileges.* You do not need a marine endorsement or a separate commercial operator permit for that amateur operation.

> **Key Information:**
> - Any FCC-issued amateur license meets the amateur licensing requirement aboard a US-registered vessel in international waters, within that license's privileges. {{< link id="E1A11" >}}
> - Station apparatus aboard a US-documented or US-registered vessel or craft must be under the physical control of an FCC-licensed amateur or a person authorized for alien reciprocal operation. {{< link id="E1A05" >}}
> - Operation aboard a ship or aircraft must have approval from the ship's master or the aircraft's pilot in command. {{< link id="E1A10" >}}

Your license establishes your amateur privileges. *Approval from the master or pilot answers a different question: whether you may use the station aboard that craft.* The station must also avoid hazards and interference to other onboard equipment. If the vessel enters waters under another country's authority, check that country's rules before operating.
