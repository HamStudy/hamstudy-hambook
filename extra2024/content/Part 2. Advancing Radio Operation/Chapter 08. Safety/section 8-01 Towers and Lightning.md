### Section 8.1: Towers and Lightning

A taller antenna is appealing when you're chasing a weak signal. Someone still has to put it up—and go back up when a connector needs attention. Tower work calls for trained people, suitable fall-protection equipment, and a rescue plan. Learning the exam answers cannot replace climbing training.

#### Staying Attached to the Tower

A **lanyard** connects a climber's harness to an anchor. A shock-absorbing lanyard helps limit the force if the climber falls. The harness, lanyard, anchor, and available clearance must work together as a fall-protection system.

> **Key Information:**
>
> - **100% tie-off** means keeping at least one lanyard attached to the tower at all times. {{< link id="E0A07" >}}
> - Attach climbing lanyards to **tower legs**. {{< link id="E0A11" >}}
> - Attach a shock-absorbing lanyard to the tower **above the climber's head level**. {{< link id="E0A12" >}}

With a suitable two-lanyard system, the climber connects one lanyard at the next approved anchor before releasing the other. *The climber stays connected throughout the move.* An anchor above the head also reduces the distance of a fall before the system begins to catch it.

The *tower-leg* answer identifies the structural member, not permission to clip onto any convenient spot. Use anchor points and equipment approved for the tower and fall-protection system. A rung, guy bracket, or antenna mast is not a substitute just because it's within reach. Follow the equipment instructions and the trained crew's work and rescue plan.

#### Give Surges a Common Path

A surge can enter on one cable and leave through another. You don't want your radio completing that path. Grounding and surge protection must work as a system to keep large voltage differences from developing across the equipment.

> **Key Information:** The primary function of an external earth connection or ground rod is **lightning charge dissipation**. {{< link id="E0A01" >}}

A **single point ground panel** brings the station's cable protectors together at a common conductive panel. Bonding means making an electrical connection between metal parts; here, those connections give the protectors a shared voltage reference during a surge.

> **Key Information:**
>
> - A single point ground panel helps ensure that all lightning protectors activate at the same time. {{< link id="E4E14" >}}
> - Install the station's AC surge protector **on the single point ground panel**. {{< link id="E4E13" >}}

The shared connection reduces dangerous voltage differences between the station's incoming lines. Bond the panel and station grounding system to the building's grounding system; separate, unconnected ground rods can create another path through the equipment. A ground rod does not replace the AC equipment-grounding conductor.

Use surge protectors suited to each line, following their installation instructions and applicable electrical codes. Have a qualified electrician handle AC wiring. These measures reduce risk, but they do not make it safe to work on the station or tower during a thunderstorm.
